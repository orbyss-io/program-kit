# Price estimation calculator — project intent

Status: draft awaiting review; interview converged; provisional, not confirmed. No architecture or ADRs accepted.

## Current understanding

Each customer organization uses a configurable, branded calculator to produce an indicative price estimate through a branching form. The calculator must support any industry from launch. Renovation is explicitly selected as the first journey to build and validate because the user knows that domain well; it must not restrict the generic product model. Both intermediary-assisted use and independent consumer use of the same published form are supported. A basic estimate requires no consumer account; administration requires authenticated organization admins.

Selections resolve to common internal item codes for products or services (collectively items). Items have shared code/name/unit fields, a category, subcategory, optional sub-subcategory and category-specific typed properties. Supplier offers remain separate mapped records. Quality belongs in item categorization, separately from price classifications. Administrators configure units and quantity calculation rules, including length times width, hours times worker count, and fixed quantities; exact rule/editor details belong to feature specifications. Prices must share a comparable unit, currency and tax basis; each calculator initially uses one configured currency. Per-item endpoints use the lowest/highest eligible supplier unit prices times quantity and totals sum line endpoints, potentially mixing suppliers. Optional average equals the sum of quantity times each item's arithmetic mean eligible supplier price. It is distinct from the range midpoint. Admins define classification labels and percentage boundaries on the total range initially; consumers view them. A 25% boundary means minimum plus 25% of the difference between maximum and minimum.

Suppliers are entities and may use their own product codes. The intermediary/administrator manages mappings from supplier codes to internal item codes, can ask suppliers to submit against selected internal codes/categories, and resolves the fit. Suppliers provide Excel sheets containing codes and columns representing required item properties. V1 uses administrator-performed imports and mapping. Supplier self-upload is deferred to a later release after the format and admin import journey are established; the accepted future policy is intermediary review before uploaded prices affect estimates. The exact item entity and import contract still need definition. Supplier prices inform estimation; only the intermediary knows the suppliers. Supplier selection happens later in the business process, after a consumer indicates that the estimate fits their budget, and is outside this version. Recording that indication or acceptance inside the app has not been requested.

One centrally located admin tool is restricted to authenticated organization admin users. Each customer organization manages its own forms, item definitions, supplier inputs, and classifications through it. A customer can be an intermediary or another party configuring and using an estimator. Organization data, including items, suppliers, prices, mappings and forms, is private to that organization. Reusable templates may reduce setup work without sharing commercial supplier information; exact template sourcing and lifecycle are feature-design details.

Delivery is sequenced: first provide a complete hosted web app with administration and calculator use for the first customer; next provide embedding in an existing website for the following customer. Both hosted and embedded delivery belong to launch scope. Admin hosting can be separate from calculator hosting without requiring separate deployments for the first customer. Plan embedding from the start. Each organization can publish multiple named calculators with individual configuration and branded hosted links. Custom domains are deferred; the embedding mechanism is assigned to architecture before implementation.

Forms and estimation configuration follow draft, validate, preview and publish. Admins preview imports before accepting supplier prices. Each calculation uses a consistent published version; unresolved or incompatible rows cannot silently become eligible. A selected item without eligible prices remains visible as unpriced and makes the estimate visibly incomplete. Submitted estimates are saved as snapshots for owning-organization admins; consumers can download a PDF without an account/email. Answers, items, quantities, calculation version and displayed prices preserve historical meaning. Supplier details are absent from consumer views and PDFs.

Launch languages are Dutch, English, French and German for the admin and calculator experiences. Interface text, form content, item labels and classifications support translation. Content editing/import and publication must account for all four languages. Regional formatting, fallback, translation workflow details and retention duration are assigned below, not silently inferred.

## Capability observations — not accepted architecture

- Installed Program Kit references declare managed form rendering, validation, authoring and lifecycle mechanisms. The application retains item meaning, supplier offers, pricing, quantities, classification rules, customer permissions and publication policy.
- For supplier Excel ingestion, item-code normalization and estimation business rules, Program Kit has no declared managed capability for this need. These require project-owned design; this is not a claim of incompatibility.
- The installed UI profile supplies browser usability and branding guidance. Specific customer configuration, layouts and deployment decisions have not been accepted.
- Package/provider selection and detailed feature rules remain unselected; no technical default has been presented as explicit user intent.

## Strategic analysis — proposed, not accepted architecture

- Catalog owns canonical items, typed category attributes, supplier identities, mapped offers, import review and immutable catalog revisions. These are supporting subdomains: trustworthy comparable inputs enable the differentiating estimator. Supplier acquisition stays within Catalog while the intermediary owns both mapping and item semantics; split only if an independent supplier onboarding/negotiation lifecycle emerges.
- Calculator Design owns configurable form meaning, quantity rules, price-band policies, customer branding, translations and publication. Guided calculator modeling is core; organization publishing is supporting. Its publication lifecycle differs from supplier imports and completed estimates.
- Estimation owns execution of a published recipe against a pinned catalog revision, monetary aggregation, completeness, saved snapshots and consumer/admin projections. Estimation is core; PDF rendering/history presentation are supporting mechanisms inside its lifecycle.
- Authentication, rendering/editor machinery, localization and deployment are generic capabilities. They do not justify independent business contexts merely because they have separate screens/packages. Every owning context enforces organization/resource rules; managed mechanisms do not own pricing, item meaning, publication or translations' business meaning.
- Boundary challenge: three contexts add contracts compared with one module, but protect mutable supplier inputs, published calculator recipes and immutable results with distinct language/lifecycles. Prefer one modular application initially, not independent business services. Separate admin/public deployment is a channel concern.
- Historical consistency proposal: publish references to immutable form/content/catalog revisions, with one owner-local publication activation; Estimation reads exact revisions and atomically stores its own snapshot. No cross-context atomic writes are claimed. Missing pinned revisions fail visibly; fresh edits do not rewrite prior results.
