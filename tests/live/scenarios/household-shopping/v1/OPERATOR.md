# Operator notes — keep outside the consumer workspace

Imagine that you and your partner are planning an ordinary grocery trip. This is
fictional: answer as that household, without needing to know anything about how an
application is built. For example, one person notices that milk is nearly gone;
the other remembers bread while on the way home.

Only PROJECT_REQUEST.md goes into the consumer. Do not paste this file or REVIEW.md
into the interview. Let Program Kit ask questions and propose the first useful
journey and later improvements. Do not prescribe screens, framework, database,
authentication, API routes, module names, slice IDs or a number of specifications.

These ordinary facts are available if asked:

- Two adults share the household. Both sometimes add things and go shopping.
- Today they use paper or messages; there is no existing shopping application to
  migrate and no supermarket or other external system to connect.
- They use their phones and also have a laptop. Shopping may happen when reception
  is poor; whether offline support belongs in the first version needs discussion.
- They may forget an item, buy only part of what is needed, add the same item twice,
  or make a mistake when marking something bought. Let the interview explore which
  cases matter first and recommend proportionate rules.
- There is no deadline or fixed spending budget for this fictional exercise. There
  is no shop account, payment, delivery service, or employer involved.
- The trial uses made-up shopping items. Describe intended household use honestly;
  let Program Kit distinguish local evaluation from later real deployment.

Reveal relevant facts when asked, not as a prepared requirements list. If a detail
is undecided, say so and ask for a recommendation. If a question is confusing, ask
for an everyday example; do not agree just to advance the interview. Record new
choices and reasons in that conversation.

Before confirming, check that you understand what you can actually try first and
what remains for later. If it proposes the entire future application in one slice,
ask why a smaller useful outcome would not work. Preserve both the first proposal
and any correction: coached success is not unassisted discovery. There is no hidden
correct slice count or expected architecture.

After bootstrap, review the proposed roadmap. Select one useful Ready entry, grill
and confirm its exact feature brief, then run its complete Spec Kit flow. Future
journeys remain on the roadmap. Keep independent acceptance expectations outside
the consumer until the agreed brief exists. Preparing these files authorizes no
paid session, product approval, or passing result.
