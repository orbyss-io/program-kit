The following preserved prototype measurements are historical development-v2/public-0.3.1 observations, not measurements of the current public 0.3.2 candidate. Measurements are seconds on this Windows host. Cold runs use isolated consumer NuGet/npm and browser caches, including actual Chromium/WebKit acquisition. The SDK, Docker engine and immutable service/host images were already installed; their preflight inventories are retained. Prepared runs reuse acquired consumer caches. Services are still fresh disposable instances. The public control is Foundation 0.3.1 with the new toolkit, not a measurement of the historical Notes custom-harness workflow. Its cold run overlapped a Python-only validator; these single observations establish no statistical speedup claim.

The stage sum adds measured generation, restore, build and service acquisition durations to the integration's monotonic clock through the verified first product operation. It excludes wrapper gaps and later native/restart/retained-read/cleanup work. The conservative bound uses `generation + full readiness wall clock - (integration elapsed - integration time to first operation)`; it retains other overhead and post-operation capability checks. The complete workflow includes subsequent tests, restart, retained read and cleanup. Base-only composition readiness takes 117.577s cold including generation and 71.761s prepared; it establishes no product operation.

The prototype-derived local development target is **at most 180s cold and 120s prepared to the first owned operation**, assessed against the conservative outer-clock bound under these stated preinstalled SDK/image conditions. All historical observed product cases meet it; current public 0.3.2 runs must be assessed independently. This is neither a factory-cold provisioning promise nor a CI wall-clock gate. Per-stage acquisition/build/service/authentication timings remain in the retained phase results.
